## Findings relevant to the research questions

### RQ1 / A1 Care decisions and controlled action
Q3, Q5 and Q6–Q9 describe uncertainty about whether watering is appropriate. The participant considers ordinary watering easy when present, but wants the reason, planned amount, recent data and an option to pause before trusting a device. This is a need for understandable decisions and control; it does not validate an integrated multi-sensor model.

### RQ2 / A7 Current alternatives
Q7 records no reported plant loss after a four-day absence. A longer absence is a concern rather than an observed failure. Q10, Q12 and Q19 retain manual care, free reminders and friends' help as potentially sufficient alternatives. The participant does not necessarily need automatic watering when present. This weakens a broad claim that every busy plant owner needs hardware automation.

### RQ3 / A2 Information and App interaction
Q11–Q12 and Q17 indicate interest in a clear current status, actionable advice, brief explanations and a stop control rather than many unexplained numbers or frequent notifications. The participant has not used a plant/device app, and no standard wireframe was shown for Q13. These are preferences, not observed usability or interface-comprehension results.

### RQ4 / A3 Hardware and adoption conditions
Q14–Q16 describe concerns about footprint, water tanks, tubes/cables near computers, cleaning, installation, stopping the device, responsibility for damage, existing-pot fit, accessories and warranty. Q15 also raises failure and reconnection behaviour. Accommodation rules remain unknown; they are not recorded as permitting or banning the device.

### RQ5 / A8 Value and purchasing conditions
Q18 gives a conditional CNY100–200 personal budget if the device fits the participant's pots and meets further conditions. Q16 and Q19 distinguish interest in a trial from a buying decision. The range is not actual payment, a market price, a paid commitment or evidence for the W3 SOM capture rate. Free tools remain a real alternative.

## Actual feedback about the instrument

The closing pilot feedback identifies similar automation questions, uncertainty about whether a question concerns current behaviour or a future device, and a feeling that the price question requires a numerical answer. The participant welcomes explicit hypothetical framing and permission to answer no purchase or uncertainty. They also want to understand failure handling and suggest retaining questions about moving and cleaning the device.

## Changes made to the guide

1. Q8 now explicitly introduces a hypothetical device and distinguishes it from past use.
2. The interviewer rule separates Q8 use situations, Q9 controls and Q12 advice sufficiency, and skips redundant probes already answered.
3. Q18 explicitly says a number is not required and permits non-purchase, uncertainty or a need for more information.
4. Q9 adds a neutral probe about conflicting advice, disconnection, reconnection and pausing. The concept states intended authorisation, limits and stop controls without claiming implemented safety.
5. Q10 includes self-watering pots to match A7. This is a research-plan coverage correction, not a claim that this participant tested a self-watering pot.
6. Questions about moving, cleaning, fitting existing pots and conditions for trial/purchase remain in the guide.

These changes are incorporated in research pack v1.3. The revised guide has not yet been re-piloted. Ask the supervisor whether a short further pilot is needed; it is not presented as a completed activity or an extra course requirement.

## Decision and limits

Keep manual confirmation and understandable care advice as hypotheses worth testing; treat automation as an optional, conditional value rather than a universal need. Retain current-alternative sufficiency and lack of a buying commitment as contrary evidence.

One student pilot does not validate working-adult or office demand, paid conversion, technical performance or market share. Continue the proposed 8–12-person exploratory round with explicit setting and tool-use coverage, subject to the supervisor's W5 evidence threshold. Do not claim that the W3 eight-person named-competitor comparison has been completed.
