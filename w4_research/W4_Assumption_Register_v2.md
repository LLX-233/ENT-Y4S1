This register carries forward the six Week 1 assumptions and adds two questions about substitutes and purchasing conditions. It is a record of uncertainties, not a list of validated findings.

## Assumptions for this interview round

### A1 Multi-sensor decisions
Some users value decisions informed by several plant-condition readings, rather than automation alone.

Status: Unvalidated.  
Research question: RQ1.  
Instrument: Q3 and Q5–Q9.  
If unsupported: Prioritise a simpler care workflow rather than claiming that more sensor inputs add user value.

### A2 App usability and trust
Clearer information and controls may improve users' understanding and trust.

Status: Unvalidated.  
Research question: RQ3.  
Instrument: Q8–Q9 and Q11–Q13.  
If unsupported: Reconsider the proposed explanation and control flow. Interviews explore attitudes; observed usability requires a consistent screen and task test.

### A3 Hardware appearance and safety
Appearance, perceived safety, footprint and maintenance may affect trial and purchase conditions.

Status: Unvalidated.  
Research question: RQ4.  
Instrument: Q14–Q16.  
If unsupported: Reprioritise hardware changes using actual user conditions. Interview opinions do not certify electrical safety or leak prevention.

### A7 Adequacy of current alternatives
Reminders, self-watering pots or timer irrigators may already meet the target user's main needs.

Status: Unvalidated; added in Week 4.  
Research question: RQ2.  
Instrument: Q4–Q7, Q10 and Q19.  
If supported: Narrow the target situation or reconsider the additional hardware value. Retain sufficient-alternative responses as contrary evidence.

### A8 Value and purchase conditions
Perceived value and the conditions under which target users would spend on Green Sight AI remain uncertain.

Status: Unvalidated; added in Week 4.  
Research question: RQ5.  
Instrument: Q4 and Q17–Q19.  
If unsupported: Do not infer a viable selling price or paid demand. Use subsequent adoption or payment tests to assess commercial feasibility.

## Technical assumptions retained from Week 1

- A4: The legacy single-threshold approach often makes unsuitable decisions in real settings.
- A5: A new model can outperform the legacy thresholds on two or three test plants.
- A6: The current sensors can support a minimum integrated decision process.

All remain unvalidated in this register. They require technical comparisons, defined controls and measured results, not interview approval. They are outside this interview round.

## Pilot indications by assumption

- A1 / RQ1: Q3 and Q6–Q9 indicate uncertainty about when to water and a conditional need for explanations and approval. They do not establish that multiple sensors outperform a simpler soil-moisture solution.
- A2 / RQ3: Q11–Q12 and Q17 indicate a preference for understandable status, brief reasons and stop controls. Q13 did not use a standard screen, so usability is untested.
- A3 / RQ4: Q14–Q16 indicate concerns about footprint, tubes/cables, leaks, cleaning, installation, existing-pot compatibility and trial safeguards. None is a technical safety result.
- A7 / RQ2: Q7, Q10, Q12 and Q19 show situations in which the participant's current routine, free reminders or help from friends may suffice. A four-day absence caused no reported loss.
- A8 / RQ5: Q18 records a conditional personal budget of CNY100–200 if the device fits the participant's pots. It is not an order, a market price estimate or evidence of actual conversion. It does not automatically replace the W3 illustrative ASP.

Retain all assumptions as unvalidated. Report these as exploratory indications from n=1, and recruit working adults and alternative-tool users before claiming coverage of those segments.
